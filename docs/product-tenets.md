# Stack Core Product Tenets

## Purpose

Stack makes useful information easy to share, understand, and return to.

A stack is a digestible guide: enough visible information to understand or act, with links providing additional depth. It should work like a concise instruction manual someone can consult whenever they need it.

## Guiding principle

**Help people find what they need in a few seconds, without requiring them to consume everything.**

A successful visit may be brief. Someone can open a restaurant guide, recognize a recommendation, check its location, and leave. Product decisions should support that outcome.

## 1. Make sharing effortless

### 1.1 Easy to access

- Public stacks open directly in a browser through a shareable link.
- Reading public information should not require an account or an app download.
- A creator's public profile should work as a useful personal website for visitors unfamiliar with Stack.

### 1.2 Easy to share

- Sharing a stack or profile should take few steps.
- Shared content should be readable and visually suitable for social platforms, including full-screen stories.
- Preserve legibility when exporting long stacks by dividing them into readable pages.
- Make the live link available alongside an export so recipients can reach the complete, current guide.

### 1.3 Easy to attribute

- Make the creator identifiable on the live stack and its exports.
- Preserve a route back to the original stack when content is shared elsewhere.
- Distinguish the person curating a guide from the sources supplying its information.
- Attribute contributions and reused content clearly where applicable.

## 2. Make information consumable

### 2.1 Easy to understand

- Communicate the guide's purpose through its title and introduction.
- Use plain language and concise entries that make sense without an accompanying video.
- Keep essential context visible; use links for deeper explanations and supporting material.

### 2.2 Easy to see and navigate

- Make entries readable, scannable, and recognizable on a phone.
- Use hierarchy, spacing, and clear link indicators to communicate structure.
- Let people find the relevant subsection without rereading the entire stack.
- Feed previews should preserve a useful reading order and clearly indicate when more content exists.

### 2.3 Easy to act on

- Include enough information to take the next step: choose a place, follow an instruction, or start a routine.
- Link labels should communicate what someone will find after tapping.
- Keep essential instructions within the stack so opening every link is unnecessary.

### 2.4 Concise by design

- Encourage short labels and focused explanations.
- Use links to add informational depth while keeping the guide digestible.
- Allow substantial collections when they remain organized and useful.
- Set text and size limits that protect readability, reliable editing, and performance.

## 3. Make useful information easy to find again

### 3.1 Easy to find

- Help people discover relevant stacks and creators through search and browsing.
- Match discovery to the content available so visitors encounter useful results.
- Favor relevant information over unnecessary interface complexity.

### 3.2 Easy to retrieve

- Help people return to a specific guide they previously used or saved.
- Use recognizable titles, stable links, and clear organization.
- Treat finding unfamiliar information and retrieving familiar information as distinct needs.

### 3.3 Easy to store

- Make saving a stack for later straightforward.
- Support portable copies that remain useful outside Stack.
- Keep creator credit, an update date, and a link to the live guide on downloads where the format allows.
- Make clear that a downloaded copy captures a version in time, while the live stack can change.

## 4. Make creation and maintenance straightforward

### 4.1 Easy to create

- Minimize the steps between an idea and a useful guide.
- Make adding text and attaching links clear.
- Help creators understand what readers will see before publishing.

### 4.2 Easy to organize

- Support meaningful grouping, ordering, and subsections.
- Let creators rearrange content without rebuilding the stack.
- Keep essential structure easy to manage as a collection grows.

### 4.3 Easy to edit and keep current

- Let owners maintain published stacks over time.
- Preserve the stack's identity and URL when content changes.
- Make it straightforward to correct text, add information, and replace broken links.
- Separate saving an edit from promoting an update to the feed.
- Communicate freshness without presenting every small edit as a new publication.

## 5. Make trust and user control clear

### 5.1 Easy to evaluate

- Help readers understand who created a guide, its sources, and when it was updated.
- Give creators room to explain relevant experience or reasons for a recommendation.
- Make commercial relationships clear when recommendations are sponsored or use affiliate links.
- Do not treat attribution or popularity as proof that information is accurate.

### 5.2 Easy to control access

- Make public, unlisted, private, and draft states understandable.
- Explain who can view a stack before the owner shares it.
- Respect those settings across profiles, feeds, search, and sharing.

### 5.3 Easy to use across circumstances

- Keep information accessible on small screens and with assistive technology.
- Favor lightweight experiences that remain useful on slower connections.
- Support reference outside the live app through suitable exports.

## Applying these tenets

These principles guide product decisions; they do not commit every possible feature to the initial release. Specific limits, layouts, and release rules belong in separate feature specifications.

When evaluating a feature or design, ask:

1. Does it help someone access, understand, share, or retrieve useful information?
2. Can someone use the relevant part without consuming the whole guide?
3. Does it preserve readability, attribution, and trust?
4. Can creators maintain the information without unnecessary work?
5. Does the value justify the added friction or complexity?

If principles conflict, protect clear access, essential understanding, and user control first. Add depth and engagement in ways that preserve those foundations.
